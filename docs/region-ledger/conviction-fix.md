**The lost-bearing "dark hairline seams"** took four probe rounds,
because the inherited diagnosis was wrong:

- The beat-repair wave had blamed the toon ramp's stops ("nulling the
  gradient map erased the lines") and buried them under ripple paint
  (±0.18). The re-critique's frame shows them untouched.
- This wave re-probed AT THE STAND: swapping the disc material's ramp
  for a softened variant (softness 0.26, then a 0.9 near-linear probe)
  changed NOTHING (pixel-diff noise only) — the ramp is exonerated.
  Hide sweeps cleared the wire carpet, the litter and the dapple.
  Per-channel toggles on the live material found it: **nulling the
  NORMAL MAP erased the lines; restoring it brought them back.** The
  sand normal's domain-warped ripple bands (70 cm grain tuned for a
  metre's read) modulate the toon terminator, and on the one surface
  smooth enough to expose it, a single meandering trough draws a dark
  hairline forty metres long.
- The fix (`Golden3Ground`, this region's material instance only):
  the perturbed normal eases back to the geometry normal across
  7–18 m of camera depth — the metre-scale grain keeps its whole
  read, and past arm's reach the paint (which already carries the
  dune's drawing) owns the surface. A drastic-normal probe verified
  the injection live; a first window (22–55 m) taught that the bowl's
  rising far slope puts the charged lines at 10–45 m, so the window
  must close by 18.
- Final read (`conv-r5` / probe `diag-r5`): the hairlines are gone at
  the stand; the tufts, ripple paint and wind bands hold the frame.
