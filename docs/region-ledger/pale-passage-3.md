## Concept

The Bone Meadows answered "life returns"; the Lantern Combs answered
"where the pale light comes from" — a kept flame, tended. This region
answers what the flame was kept FOR: **the diver comes out from behind
the paper into the morning itself.** Dawn at the bottom of a pale sea
is not a sky event — the horizon lightens first, colour returns to the
ground, and the sun rises OUT OF THE GROUND: at the spoke's own
bearing THE DAYSPRING stands — a half-risen pearl sun cresting the
last terraces, the painted morning burning behind it, and THE
CHORISTER (a great white sea-angel) rising and falling before it like
a bird greeting the sun. The pass lands in THE UNDAWN — the hour
before, the region's own held-breath dark — so the country past it
opens as daybreak. Arrival, not spectacle: the strictest-hush province
ends on a held major chord.

The story number is `dawn(u, v)` in [0, 1] — how much of the morning
has reached this point: 0 at the threshold (the Combs' lamp-warmth
handed over and fading), 1 at the Dayspring. Unlike the Combs' `lumen`
(a kept flame at a point) it is a SUNRISE gradient: it arrives along
the spoke, from ahead. Ground warmth, blush, garden density, tower
flank warmth and the light grammar all read this one number; the mere
is held a step cooler by hand (the mirror keeps the hour before).
